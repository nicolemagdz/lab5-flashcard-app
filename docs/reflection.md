### Reflection

Throughout this assignment, I acted as both the architect and editor by making decisions about how the frontend, backend, database, shared code, and tests should work together while also reviewing, debugging, and correcting the AI-generated code when it did not work as expected. One of the biggest problems I encountered was getting the integration tests to work correctly across the backend and database layers, which required me to trace issues through the controllers, services, Prisma, module imports, Vitest configuration, and mocking. Working through these problems improved the reliability of the application because I was not simply accepting AI-generated code; I was testing it, identifying failures, and making sure the different parts of the stack actually worked together. The process was also extremely frustrating because I spent more than 12 hours working on the assignment in one day, and not to mention, I had started the assignment on Tuesday of the week, meaning I had spent more than 24 combined hours on it. A large portion of that time was spent troubleshooting issues that were not immediately obvious, which showed me that although AI can significantly speed up writing code, human oversight, architectural decision-making, editing, and debugging are still necessary to produce reliable software. It was also a great way for me to be angry with AI so I do not fully and completely rely on it.
